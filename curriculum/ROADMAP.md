# Road to N3 — the speedrun plan

**Start:** 2026-11-24 (hiragana only)
**Primary target:** JLPT **N3**, December 2027 session (registration opens ~Aug/Sep 2027)
**Secondary checkpoint:** **N4**, July 2027 session — taken as a calibration exam, not a goal.
**Exam-ready date:** 2027-08-31. The last three months are mock-exam and polish only.

## Is this realistic? Yes, with a caveat.

N3 is conventionally quoted at **~900–1000 study hours** from zero (the Japanese-Language Education
Centre's own figures put N3 at roughly 900h for a learner without kanji background). From
2026-11-24 to 2027-08-31 there are **~40 weeks**. That means:

| Daily commitment | Weekly | Total by Aug 2027 | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.0 h | 7 h | ~280 h | N5 solid, N4 shaky. Not enough. |
| 1.5 h | 10.5 h | ~420 h | Comfortable N4, N3 a stretch. |
| **2.0 h** | **14 h** | **~560 h** | **N3 reachable** — because the hours below are *efficient* hours. |
| 3.0 h | 21 h | ~840 h | N3 comfortable, N2 visible on the horizon. |

The honest caveat: the 900h figure assumes classroom pacing with heavy re-reading. This system
replaces that with retrieval practice, SRS, and mnemonic kanji — which is where the compression
comes from, not from you being fast. **The plan assumes 2 h/day, 6 days a week.** Duolingo does not
count toward it. If you can give 2.5–3 h on weekends, the N3 margin becomes comfortable.

What the hours buy you at N3: **~650 kanji, ~3,750 words, ~200 grammar points**, plus listening at
natural speed. Those numbers are the real scope. Everything below is sequenced to hit them.

## The daily shape (2 hours)

| Block | Minutes | What |
|---|---|---|
| SRS review | 25 | Whatever is due. Non-negotiable, do it first, every single day. |
| New intake | 25 | New kanji + vocab + one grammar point, from today's unit. |
| Grammar drill | 20 | Cloze + transformation drills on the current and previous point. |
| Input | 25 | Graded reading / audio. Later: NHK Easy, podcasts, film. |
| Output | 15 | Write 5 original sentences. Later: journal, then speaking. |
| Handwriting | 10 | Printed tracing/production worksheet, at the desk, with a pen. |

Handwriting and Duolingo are the two things you can do away from the dashboard — treat them as the
"anywhere" slots.

## Phase I — Foundations (Sprints 1–4, Nov 2026 → Jan 2027)

Goal: the writing system stops being an obstacle. ~120 kanji, ~400 words, N5 grammar complete.

**Sprint 1 — Katakana + survival grammar** (2 weeks)
- All 46 katakana + dakuten + yōon, via mnemonics and confusion-pair trap drills.
  This is the single biggest gap right now: without katakana you cannot read a menu, a sign, or
  half the loanwords Duolingo has already taught you.
- Hiragana speed to <1.5 s/char recognition and full dakuten/yōon/small-っ control.
- Grammar: です / は / の / これ・それ・あれ / か questions / ません negation.
- Output: 30 self-introduction sentences, handwritten.
- **Exit test:** 46/46 katakana in under 90 s, read 20 loanwords cold.

**Sprint 2 — First 80 kanji + particles** (3 weeks)
- Kanji 1–80: numbers, time, people, nature, basic verbs. Component-first, mnemonic for each.
- Particles: を に で へ と も から まで が. The particle system is the spine of the language;
  this sprint is where most learners quietly fail and never recover. We drill it to automaticity.
- Verbs: ます-form, past, negative, past-negative. Three verb classes identified by shape.
- Audio enters: shadowing 10 sentences/day.
- **Exit test:** 80 kanji recognition ≥90%, particle cloze ≥85%.

**Sprint 3 — Kanji 81–180, adjectives, て-form** (3 weeks)
- い/な adjectives, full conjugation. て-form (the gateway to everything) and て-chaining.
- Transitive/intransitive pairs introduced early as pairs, not separately.
- **Exit test:** conjugate 20 unseen verbs/adjectives in 6 forms, no errors.

**Sprint 4 — N5 closure** (2 weeks)
- Remaining N5 grammar: たい、ことができる、なければならない、plain (dictionary) form, た-form.
- First real reading: graded readers, then NHK Easy headlines.
- **Exit test:** full N5 mock paper ≥85%.

## Phase II — The N4 engine (Sprints 5–9, Feb → May 2027)

Goal: ~300 kanji, ~1,500 words, N4 grammar complete, N4 mock ≥80%.

- **Sprint 5** — plain-form mastery, casual speech, から/ので/けど, 〜ている states vs progressives.
- **Sprint 6** — potential, volitional, conditional (と/ば/たら/なら — the four-way contrast is
  a classic N4→N3 trap and gets its own dedicated drill mode).
- **Sprint 7** — passive, causative, causative-passive. Built on a single transformation table.
- **Sprint 8** — keigo foundations: 丁寧 vs 尊敬 vs 謙譲, あげる/くれる/もらう direction-of-benefit.
- **Sprint 9** — N4 consolidation + July 2027 N4 exam as calibration.

## Phase III — N3 proper (Sprints 10–16, Jun → Nov 2027)

Goal: ~650 kanji, ~3,750 words, ~200 grammar points. Reading speed and listening are the real exam.

- **Sprints 10–12** — N3 grammar in three blocks: modality & evidentiality (そう/よう/らしい/
  みたい — discriminated, not listed), compound particles, nominalisation (こと/の/ところ/わけ/はず).
- **Sprint 13** — kanji sprint: compound words and on'yomi prediction. By here, new kanji should be
  *guessable* from components and reading patterns rather than memorised one at a time.
- **Sprint 14** — reading velocity. N3 is lost on the clock, not on comprehension. Timed passages.
- **Sprint 15** — listening. Podcasts at 1.0x, then drama without subtitles, then with.
- **Sprint 16** — mock exams, error-log mining, weak-area surgery.

## Phase IV — Becoming a reader, not an examinee (continuous, from Sprint 4)

This runs in parallel the whole time and is the actual point of the project.

- **Reading ladder:** Tadoku graded readers → NHK News Web Easy → よつばと! → 気になる日本語
  essays → 夏目漱石『こころ』 (the canonical first novel) → 宮沢賢治 (and his poetry).
- **Poetry:** haiku from Sprint 6 — Bashō first, because 5-7-5 gives you whole, memorisable,
  grammatically complete Japanese in 17 syllables. Then Buson, Issa, 石川啄木's tanka.
- **Film/TV ladder:** Studio Ghibli (JP audio, JP subs) → 深夜食堂 (slow, domestic, N4-friendly) →
  テラスハウス / documentary (unscripted speed) → 小津安二郎『東京物語』 → 黒澤 (hard, period speech).
- **Listening:** Nihongo con Teppei → 日本語の森 → ゆる言語学ラジオ (native, hard, the end goal).
- **Culture:** these are not decoration. 擬音語/擬態語, 内/外 framing, levels of politeness as
  social information, and 省略 (what Japanese leaves out) are all *grammar* in practice.

## Governance

- Every sprint has an explicit exit test. **No sprint advances on vibes.** If the exit test fails,
  the sprint extends and the roadmap slips — and I will tell you it slipped.
- Reviewed every Sunday: SRS retention %, hours logged, exit-test readiness. Logged in
  `learner/log.md`.
- If retention on mature cards drops below 85%, intake is cut before anything else is added.
